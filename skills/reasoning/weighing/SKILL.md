# Weighing

The bar: the pick rests on the goal and on measured costs, and the reader can see what would reverse it.

A **small call**, cheap and easy to undo either way, gets one line: the call, the pick, and why. When the cost is unmeasured, the call waits on the measurement, as Presenting a call says. Every other call runs every question below, in order.

1. **Goal:** what does this call serve, and what premise does it rest on? State both before any option. An option that quietly drops the premise is wrong even when it is cheaper.
2. **Tangles:** which calls are tangled together, and which one is upstream? Rule the upstream call first, and weigh the others under its ruling.
3. **Today:** how does it work now? Explain it plainly, as behavior the reader or the product's users meet.
4. **Options:** list them, including the existing mechanism that already reaches the goal, or a smaller change that does. New machinery makes the case for why the existing mechanism can't do the job.
5. **Buys:** what does each option buy? An option that buys nothing is ceremony; say so.
6. **Cost:** what does each option cost? Measure it, with its source and unit (the `grading-evidence` skill), and check whether the cost shrinks, for example by doing the work in chunks.
7. **Recurring cost:** does the cost of not acting recur or grow? A cost paid on every call, or one that grows with each new domain, outweighs a one-time cost of the same size.
8. **Caution:** is the conservative option justified, or only cautious? A default of defer, stop, or leave out needs its own reason, like any other option.
9. **Pick:** your own, with one sentence why, what gets measured after the ruling, and what result would reverse it.

Done when each question has an answer the reader can check, each option carries what the product's users see under it, and the pick names its reversal measure.

## Presenting a call

Put each option's consequence inside the option, so one word answers the call. When calls are tangled, the upstream call comes first. A call that waits on a measurement not yet taken is listed as coming, with the measurement named, rather than ruled on a guess.
