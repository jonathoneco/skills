# Talk

A talk is one page the user presents from, scrolling, talking over it. The audience never reads it; it is the deck behind the voice. It is always one page.

## Reasoning

Each answer is a beat: name the slide that carries it, or say in the notes why this talk does without it.

1. **Stake.** What does the audience lose, or miss, without this, in their own terms (time, money, risk, a decision they can't make)? Done when each stake has its source and date window; it opens the talk.
2. **Baseline.** Say what the system is today, bluntly. Done when each stake from step 1 is derived from that shape.
3. **Payoffs.** Find what answers each stake. Done when each stake has its answer.
4. **Belief.** What must this audience believe to act? Done when each belief is one sentence, and names the payoff or evidence that carries it.
5. **Doubt.** What would make them doubt each belief, given what they have seen and run? Answer each doubt with what settles it for them. The answer may call for a before and after of the things and their connections, drawn at the object level with every edge real; what fails, and where, when someone does it wrong; or the honest scale and the planned work it rides. Done when each doubt has its answer on a slide, or is named on a slide as open.
6. **Next.** What does the audience do next, from the work they have in flight? Done when the last slide names that action, who takes it, and where they start.

**The takeaway carries** the stake, the change that answers it, and what the audience does next.

## Form

- One Heading 2 per slide, and the heading is the slide's claim in one sentence. A divider ends every slide, so one scroll moves one slide.
- Under the heading: at most three support lines and at most one visual, such as a key figure in a callout, a two-column before and after, an HTML block, or a short table. Read the slide aloud and time it: under twenty seconds. Overflow means cut or split, never a smaller visual.
- Vary the visuals. Three uniform bullet slides in a row is the ceiling; a section's biggest claim renders big, in a callout, not bulleted.
- No toggles: a presenter can't open one mid-sentence.
- The slide copy argues to a close: pains mirror payoffs, and the last slide resolves. Leave in the threads the argument ties off, and keep out the oblique tangents prose can afford.

## Agent context

Light: the source and date window behind each number, and the shipped work each example decomposes.
